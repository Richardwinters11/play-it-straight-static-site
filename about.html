About
the Play

"Students. Teachers. Administrators. Parents. Politicians. All vying for authority. Using schools as pawns on the political chessboard. Prayer on the 50-yard line. New laws. New board members. New policies. Diversity, Equity, Inclusion. Critical Race Theory. Don't Say Gay. Woke. Access to bathrooms. Which books? Which movies? What language? Winter Break or Christmas Break? I could go on. And on and on and on…Who's in control here? You tell me"

— Ross, Act Two, Scene Two
01 — Synopsis

What happens.

Mr. Ross tells his drama students to dig deep, speak from the heart, and embrace the chaos of creativity. Then, one day the chaos slips its leash, as a careless remark to a disruptive student is out of his mouth before he can stop it.

By the next morning, a parent has heard. By afternoon, every kid in advanced drama has been pulled into the office and questioned. The inquiry turns up nothing — until Hope, Ross's teaching assistant, defends him: his classroom is the only place at school where she feels safe to be herself. "It's the only class I have with other gay kids," she says.

That single sentence, meant as a compliment, lands on the wrong desk. Policies collide, careers unravel, and the people with the least power turn out to have the sharpest instincts.
