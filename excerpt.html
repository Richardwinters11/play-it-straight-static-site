Selected scenes from Play It Straight. For a full perusal script, contact the playwrights.

Act One — Opening
Lights up to reveal ROSS, a high school Drama teacher, standing in front of what appear to be 3 dead students. JENNY lies in state, on a funeral bier; IVAN is sprawled downstage of her, his face gory with blood which has gushed from his mouth; HOPE lies nearby, a cup in her hand, a dagger and an open binder just downstage of her head.
ROSS
Most important thing about teaching? Control the classroom. If you can't do that, nothing else you do is going to matter.
(gestures at the corpses)
This, right here? This is perfect teaching moment.  Everyone is on task yet completely under control. It won't last.  Nor should it.  Things are always better and much more interesting when a little chaos creeps in.  It's where all the magic happens. But let's just take a moment to enjoy it anyway, shall we?
(deep, meditative breath)
This is nice.
Act One — The Complaint
Administrative office, the next morning. HEWITT, the new Assistant Principal — fresh off her first week of her first admin job — is alone. STERN, a uniformed Deputy Sheriff, has come in person, unannounced, demanding to speak with the Principal.
Stern
Yesterday afternoon, David Isaac Ross, the man who runs your theatre program, asked my son to sit on his lap.
(beat)
My son is a sophomore — he's 15 years old — I want him removed from that class, effective immediately. And, I am recommending that you remove Mr. Ross from the classroom as well, and initiate an independent investigation into his misconduct. Did you get all that?
Hewitt
Mr. Stern, it sounds like you're making a lot of assumptions based on circumstantial—
Stern
Based on experience, ma'am. I've been in law enforcement over twenty years — I've seen some things. You learn to trust your gut.
Hewitt
Well, I don't have the benefit of your experience, so I need you to be explicit. What, exactly, do you imagine your gut is telling you about Mr. Ross?
Stern
Put it this way: If I asked you to sit on my lap right now — what would your gut be telling you about me?
Pause.


Act Two — The Flashback
A prep period, months earlier. Between a lockdown drill and the bell, HOPE tells ROSS about a painting on a kids' Bible her parents gave her when she was four — a smiling Jesus holding a lamb, surrounded by kids.
Hope
It was cute! Of course it was white Jesus, so I just thought, that's my Dad. Probably because he's so good with little kids. He used to get me laughing so hard I couldn't breathe — and then I got older and watched him do the same thing with CJ — he's amazing at it. With toddlers.
(beat)
Teenagers, not so much. You start having a mind of your own, and he's way out of his comfort zone. If Mom hadn't left — it'd be easier.
Ross
Where'd she go?
Hope
Cincinnati, her sister lives there. Then she kept going. 
Silence.
Hope
And then it seemed like Dad was mad all the time. All I know is, he doesn't make us laugh very much anymore. He saves the humor for his "online community."
(a difficult admission)
I'll come out to him someday, but not while I'm living under his roof. Nooo thanks! I'd rather just… let him think I'm who he thinks I am.
