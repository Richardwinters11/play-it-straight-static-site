01 — The Play

A comedy about the impossible job of teaching teenagers to tell the truth.

Ross has been teaching high school drama for longer than most of his students have been alive, and he'd tell you he's pretty good at it — as long as nobody gets in his way.

Then a throwaway sarcastic line in rehearsal gets reported to a deputy sheriff. A new assistant principal discovers her district has a mandatory outing policy. A student's Bible painting of a smiling Jesus collides with online rage-radio. And the Spring production of Romeo and Juliet starts falling apart in ways Shakespeare never imagined.

Written by three theater makers with decades of classroom experience between them, Play It Straight looks at what happens when the adults who are supposed to be in charge are the ones most out of their depth — and the kids, for once, are the ones holding the line.

It's a play about control. About chaos. About whether the two can share a room without killing each other. And about what a teacher owes to a student when the law says one thing and the kid's life says another.

02 — From the Script
Lights up to reveal ROSS, a high school Drama teacher, standing in front of what appear to be 3 dead students. JENNY lies in state, on a funeral bier; IVAN is sprawled downstage of her, his face gory with blood which has gushed from his mouth; HOPE lies nearby, a cup in her hand, a dagger and an open binder just downstage of her head.
ROSS
Most important thing about teaching? Control the classroom. If you can't do that, nothing else you do is gonna matter. (gestures at the corpses) This, right here? First time I've felt in control since last Tuesday. It won't last, but can we just take a moment to enjoy it together? (deep, meditative breath) This is nice.
